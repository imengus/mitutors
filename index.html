Our strategies

Over our experience, we learned three main things:

  • 1 · Knowledge
  • 2 · Articulation
  • 3 · Habits

1Knowledge

Know deeper and broader than other candidates

Dive into areas that feel profound to you. Interviewers will take notice.

What to do

In our words

Start from the basics

  • YouTube videos and articles outlining the interview process
  • How MMIs and panel interviews work
  • The main question types you will face
Mete

Needless to say, I knew very little to begin with.

Use YouTube for second-hand experience

  • Doctors narrating their daily plan and incidents
  • Doctors’ views on the shortcomings of medicine and the NHS
  • Patients: what are their biggest (dis)satisfactions?
  • Lived-experience of patients that touches you, so your reasons for medicine are emotionally grounded
  • Recorded ward rounds and consultations to get a feel for it.
Ilkin

I found it interesting to watch psychiatric patients describe how doctors failed to treat their unique condition. Also neurosurgeries.

Read and digest

  • Medical ethics textbooks (the basics)
  • Articles: BMJGuardian Health
  • Landmark cases: e.g. GillickBolamRe C
  • MPTS Tribunal proceedings against malpractising doctors
Ilkin

Medicine is very deep. The more I got curious about its depth, the more satisfaction I felt in going deeper. Without stimulating my curiosity, my brain wouldn't have had the focus to think deeper.

Ilkin

Building your knowledge base will only feel like drudgery if you’re doing it in the wrong way.

Reflect on your own experience

  • Return to your volunteering and work experience
  • Dissect it in depth to find talking points

Make structured notes

  • Group them by question topic
  • Learn them as you would for an exam

Research beyond the course

  • The university and the city
  • The course itself
  • Your career progression
Mete

Examiners can tell very quickly which students have put in the time and which haven’t. With good practice, it will be obvious to them that you have.

2Articulation

Learn to think deeply on the spot

A good interview answer is always reasoned from scratch.

What to do

In our words

Learn the shape of an answer, not the answer

  • How to think through and structure answers for each question type
  • What examiners look for: their marking criteria
  • Note taking is easy, but turning them into coherent speech is what needs strategy
Mete

No question in any interview can be answered from memory.

Mete

Notes that read well don’t always sound right when spoken.

Practise on your toes

  • Record your answers, and give honest feedback to yourself.
  • Ask family and friends to put you in unfamiliar situations
  • Always use unpredictable questions, so you learn step by step reasoning on the spot
  • Even the answers you are preparing talking points for, get used to saying it in a new way each time
Ilkin

Even if it is cringe-inducing, you must analyse with painstaking honesty. If you don't address your shortcomings, then who will?

Ilkin

Initially you may need a lot of time to think and craft your responses, but over time you will become surprisingly good at answering without thinking. We want you to be a natural.

Practise with experienced people

  • Students from your desired medical schools
  • Or anyone who has been through the process
  • They know what their examiners want

Get detailed feedback, and repeat

  • Repetition plus tailored feedback builds confidence, fluency and answer quality
  • Feedback shows you what level you are working at
Ilkin

Unless you receive detailed feedback, you cannot know what level you are working at. Either you will be overconfident, or you will underestimate your strength. Both are a tragedy in an interview.

Simulate the real thing

  • Mock MMIs and panel interviews in each school’s format
  • Under timed conditions
  • Over time, it just feels like sitting a past paper under exam conditions
Ilkin

The more mock practice you do, the more you feel "ah, it's not so bad as I thought"

3Habits

Build healthy habits in advance of the day

Confidence and soft-skills stabilise with long-term habits.

What to do

In our words

Set a standard and a rhythm

  • No cutting corners: hold yourself, and ask others to hold you, to a high standard
  • A weekly schedule that demands regular output
  • A few hours every day, spread over months
Ilkin

A few hours every day spread out over months means you jump level. You surpass mediocrity. You become a superstudent.

Study for it like your exams

  • Get as much experience as possible
  • Expect to be shaky at first: soft-skills take repeated effort
Mete

Get as much experience as possible, and study for it in the same way you study for your exams.

Mete

Don’t be demoralised if you’re shaky to begin with. It takes time, and we are here to support you every step of the way.

Look after yourself

  • Meaningful social contact to keep your human side alive
  • Regular sleep and exercise for grounded thinking
  • Feel well, so you can show your competence
Ilkin

Ideally you will feel purpose and satisfaction from this process, otherwise you will burn out or quit.

Make your technique automatic

  • Pause before answering
  • Speak slower and keep composure
  • Key phrases, plus relaxation and delay techniques to gather your thoughts
  • Keep a phrase ready to buy a second: “That’s an interesting question, let me take a second to consider it.”
Ilkin

It all happens automatically to the level of your preparation and confidence. You can cultivate both with the right guidance and consistency!

Plan the day itself

  • Accept that there is little that you can do. Try to enjoy, and if you're stressed that's good as well. It shows that you care for yourself and your future
  • Rule: There will be at least one thing that goes wrong.
  • As long as you feel 60% confident, it usually works out
  • Don’t cram the night before
  • Even better with a good night’s sleep and a tidy room
  • Look after your health so even if one thing goes wrong, you can face the rest
Ilkin

The night before my UCL MMI I had to sleep on my friend's floor so I got no sleep. What got me the offer were 2 things: my level of preparation that did the speaking for me, and the fact I was in a good space in the previous weeks.

Mete

At Sheffield I was asked what I was looking forward to about living in Sheffield as a student. I blanked and said visiting the steel factories. Not to worry, interviewers give more weight to certain mistakes than a simple slip. I moved on and got my offer!
